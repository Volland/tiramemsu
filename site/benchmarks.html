Benchmarks: SQLite, redb and Oxigraph

tiramemsu 0.8.1 on SQLite (with and without its LFTJ operator) and on redb, against Oxigraph 0.5 on RocksDB, the reference Rust RDF store. Same generated data, same queries, every answer compared across engines before anything is timed. The losses are on this page too.

Cycles

Worst-case-optimal joins

A triangle through hubs at 10⁶ statements: 20.76 ms on redb, 29.00 ms on SQLite with LFTJ, 4.87 s on SQLite's own plan and 55.35 s on Oxigraph.

Joins

Chains, stars and Cypher

The snowflake F1 at 10⁶: 20.22 ms on redb, 23.17 ms on SQLite, 179.34 ms on Oxigraph. A Cypher two-hop from a property map: 379 µs on redb, 14.44 ms on SQLite.

Readers

Concurrent lookups

Two-hop lookups per second on 16 threads at 10⁶: 67 577 on Oxigraph, 49 496 on redb, 15 897 on SQLite, whose reader pool tops out early.

Writes

Transactions cost time

One insert at 10⁵: 5 µs on Oxigraph against 111 µs on SQLite and 120 µs on redb. Bulk load of a million statements: 2.0 s, 51 s and 89 s.

Read these numbers with our priorities

  • Transactions before speed. Every tiramemsu write is one ACID transaction with a statement id, a transaction number, schema checks, an event, full history and cascading retractions. The write columns compare different amounts of work.
  • Durability: both tiramemsu engines run at Normal (SQLite synchronous = NORMAL with WAL; redb group commit within 50 ms or 1 000 commits); Oxigraph at its default, RocksDB with its write-ahead log.
  • Correctness before timing: each query runs once on every engine and the results are compared (counts always, sorted rows up to 1 000); a mismatch stops the run. A query that passes 60 s on its checked run is a timeout and takes no part in that comparison. The cap bounds the checked run only, so a timed median can exceed it.
  • What each engine can answer: Oxigraph has no transaction or valid time, no statement ids and no Cypher, so it is left out of the time-view lookups, Cypher, Cypher paths, cascade and supersede (“—”).

Known caveats. At 10⁶ statements only redb answered the skewed diamond and 4-cycle, so those two answers were not cross-checked in this run; the differential tests check the same queries on small graphs. Concurrent readers moved −17 % (4 threads) at 10⁵ and +20 % at 10⁶ against the previous run with no change on their path; read differences of that size as noise between runs.
